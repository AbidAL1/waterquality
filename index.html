Health Impact — Waterborne Diseases
Diseases linked to contaminated drinking water · Special focus on women & children in rural Eastern UP
Chemical ContaminationChronic
👶
Methemoglobinemia (Blue Baby Syndrome)
Cause: Nitrate >45 mg/L
Nitrate converts haemoglobin in infants, reducing oxygen. Kaushambi Nitrate = 146 mg/L — 3.24× BIS. Can be fatal in infants under 6 months.
CRITICAL · Infants <6 months
🦷
Dental & Skeletal Fluorosis
Cause: Fluoride >1.0 mg/L
Mottling of teeth and bone deformation. Kaushambi Fluoride = 1.27 mg/L. Pregnant women and developing children most affected.
HIGH RISK · Children + Pregnant Women
🫘
Kidney Stones (Urolithiasis)
Cause: Hardness >200 mg/L + TDS >500 mg/L
High calcium/magnesium promotes stone formation. Worst blocks: Kaushambi 500, Ghazipur 439, Pratapgarh 430 mg/L.
MODERATE · Adults + Elderly
🫁
Arsenicosis
Cause: Arsenic >0.01 mg/L
Skin lesions, neuropathy, and cancers from long-term arsenic. Eastern UP Ganga belt is in India's high-arsenic zone.
HIGH RISK · Long-term exposure
Microbial ContaminationAcute
🦠
Acute Diarrhoeal Disease
Cause: Low FRC + High Turbidity
Insufficient chlorine + turbidity enables bacterial growth. Balkaranpur FRC=0.05, Karenti FRC=0.00. Leading cause of under-5 mortality in rural UP.
CRITICAL · Under-5 Mortality Risk
💛
Typhoid Fever
Cause: Salmonella typhi in contaminated water
Zero FRC = zero protection. High turbidity provides bacterial hiding places. Pregnant women risk miscarriage.
HIGH RISK · Women + Children
🫀
Hepatitis E
Cause: Viral, faecal-oral route
Maternal mortality 15–25% in third trimester. Karenti FRC=0.00 — maximum vulnerability. Most dangerous for pregnant women.
CRITICAL · Pregnant Women
🩸
Anaemia & Worm Infections
Cause: Helminths + nutritional deficiency
Roundworm/hookworm cause iron-deficiency anaemia. ~60% UP women anaemic (NFHS-5). Compounded by waterborne parasites.
HIGH RISK · Women + School Children

AI Intelligence Brief
Research synthesis — methodology, findings, and recommendations
Project Overview
CST-UP/DST funded project on AI-Enhanced Drinking Water Quality Monitoring in Rural Eastern Uttar Pradesh, IIIT Allahabad. Hierarchical K-Means clustering on secondary data (CGWB, JJM) identifies most water-vulnerable communities for primary field data collection, with focus on women and children.
Methodology
Three-level hierarchical K-Means (K=3). Level 1: 27 districts, 6 parameters. Level 2: Blocks in worst 3 districts, 5 parameters. Level 3: Villages in worst blocks, 8–9 parameters including FRC as inverse risk. Weighted composite risk scores vs BIS IS 10500:2012. Missing values: column-wise median imputation (60% exclusion threshold). Optimal K via Elbow Method.
Most Critical Finding
Karenti village (Kunda, Pratapgarh) — Risk Score 59.01. FRC = 0.00 mg/L (zero disinfection) + Turbidity 4.11 NTU (4.11× BIS). Five parameters simultaneously exceeding BIS limits. Highest microbial risk of all three field sites — priority for immediate intervention.
Women & Children Vulnerability
• Nitrate (Kaushambi 146 mg/L) → Blue Baby Syndrome in infants <6 months
• Fluoride (1.27 mg/L) → fluorosis in children + pregnant women
• FRC ≈ 0 in all 3 village sites → Hepatitis E (15–25% maternal mortality in 3rd trimester)
• High turbidity → diarrhoea → leading cause of under-5 mortality in rural UP
Field Visit Priority
1. Karenti (Site #3) — FRC=0, 5 params exceeding, Score 59.01
2. Balkaranpur (Site #1) — FRC critical, 4 params exceeding, Score 24.46
3. Gain (Site #2) — Turbidity + FRC, Score 17.60

Collect: water samples, household survey, GPS coordinates, women/children health indicators, primary water source documentation.
Research Paper Citation Block
"A hierarchical K-Means clustering approach (K=3) was employed for secondary data analysis of 27 districts of Eastern Uttar Pradesh. Weighted composite risk scores were computed using BIS IS 10500:2012 limits (Fluoride, Nitrate: w=3; EC, Hardness, TDS: w=2; pH: w=1). Z-score standardisation applied prior to clustering. Optimal K by Elbow Method. Three field visit sites identified: Balkaranpur (Kaushambi), Gain (Ghazipur), Karenti (Pratapgarh)."
